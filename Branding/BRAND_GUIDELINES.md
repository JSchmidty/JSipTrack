## 1. Brand Strategy

### 1.1 — Positioning Statement

SipTrack is the only alcohol awareness app designed to be equally at home in a recovery meeting and at a dinner party — giving every person, regardless of their relationship with alcohol, the data they need to make informed decisions.

---

### 1.2 — Mission Statement

SipTrack exists to give every person — regardless of age, culture, or reason — clear, accurate information about alcohol's effect on their body. We believe awareness is the foundation of every good decision. We don't tell people what to choose; we make sure they always know what they're choosing.

---

### 1.3 — Brand Personality

**Five adjectives that define SipTrack:**

**1. Trustworthy**
SipTrack's core job is to be believed. The BAC estimate shown on screen is the one thing the user needs to trust completely. Trustworthy means: scientific grounding (Widmark formula), transparent disclaimers, no exaggeration. When in doubt, be more conservative — not more alarming. Trust is the product.

**2. Calm**
The app often opens in high-stakes moments — someone at a party wondering if they're okay to drive, a person checking in on their recovery streak, a nurse checking before medication. Panic is the enemy. Every color, every animation, every word choice should lower the heart rate, not raise it. Calm is not the same as cold — it's confident, settled, unflappable.

**3. Inclusive**
SipTrack is for everyone: old and young, any ethnicity, any gender, any relationship with alcohol. This isn't a marketing claim — it is an engineering constraint. If a single persona in the validated set of 25 feels this app "isn't for them," that is a design failure. Inclusive means deliberately reflecting all of them.

**4. Precise**
SipTrack deals in numbers. BAC to two decimal places. Time remaining in hours and minutes. Drink count. Session duration. Precision earns trust. It signals: we take this seriously, and so should you. Precision extends to copy (exact, specific language), to design (pixel-perfect layouts), and to data (calibrated estimates, not guesses).

**5. Empowering**
Not prescriptive. Not preachy. SipTrack never tells users what to do — it gives them what they need to decide for themselves. Empowerment means the user feels more capable after using the app than before. It means the data belongs to them, not to us. It means we celebrate their choices, whatever those choices are.

---

### 1.4 — Brand Promise

**"We give you the data. What you do with it is entirely yours."**

Every SipTrack user — active drinker, moderate drinker, person in recovery, designated driver, medical professional — receives the same promise: accurate information, privately stored, never judged, always available when you need it.

---

### 1.5 — Taglines

Five options with rationale:

**Option A: "Know Your Number."**
Rationale: Simple, direct, WCAG-level readability. Works for every persona — whether "your number" is a BAC reading or a sobriety day count. Passes the Bob Test (immediately understood at 67). Passes the Terrence Test (not about drinking — about a number).

**Option B: "Your body. Your data. Your call."**
Rationale: Three-word rhythm, empowerment focus, privacy-first signal. The "your call" ending is non-prescriptive. Works in medical contexts ("your data") and recovery contexts ("your call"). Slightly long for watch complications.

**Option C: "Awareness changes everything."**
Rationale: Aspirational, abstract, avoids any mention of alcohol. Works for recovery, medical, and wellness contexts. Could apply to many health categories — less ownable as a result.

**Option D: "Clear data. Clear decisions."**
Rationale: Functional, professional, appeals to Data Trackers and Medical segments. Clean rhythm. Passes the Dr. Sharma Test immediately. Less warmth for Recovery segment.

**Option E: "Be in the know."**
Rationale: Casual, friendly, college-safe. Destiny would put this on a widget without embarrassment. Terrence reads it as: "be informed about yourself." Broad enough not to imply drinking culture. Slightly generic.

**→ Primary Recommended Tagline: "Know Your Number."**

Rationale for selection: The fewest words. The broadest meaning. BAC to two decimal places, sobriety streak days, drink count — every SipTrack metric is a number. It's imperious without being commanding, personal without being intimate, and neutral without being empty. Works at 16px on a Watch complication and at 120px on a billboard. Passes all 10 persona stress tests.

**Secondary Tagline (recovery/wellness contexts):** "Be in the know."

## 2. Voice & Tone Guide

### 2.1 — The Four Rules of SipTrack Copy

**Rule 1: Never glamorize drinking. Never shame it.**
SipTrack copy is a Switzerland — completely neutral on the subject of alcohol as a lifestyle choice. We do not use language that makes drinking sound fun, sophisticated, or exciting. We also do not use language that implies drinking is shameful, dangerous by default, or a problem to be solved. Every word is a tool for information, not persuasion.

**Rule 2: Works for both active drinkers and people in recovery, on the same screen.**
This is the hardest constraint and the most important. Every interface element — every notification, every status message, every onboarding step — must be written such that someone 14 months sober reads it without feeling triggered or excluded, AND someone who just had two glasses of wine reads it without feeling judged. When in doubt: default to information, not interpretation.

**Rule 3: 6th-grade reading level for all user-facing copy.**
Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level should target 5.0–6.5 for all in-app copy. This is not dumbing down — it is respecting that users read on small screens, in dim lighting, after a long day, sometimes when they are emotionally activated. Short sentences. Active voice. Anglo-Saxon words over Latinate ones. Test every line: would a 12-year-old understand this immediately?

**Rule 4: Explain BAC on first use; prefer visuals over "standard drink."**
Blood Alcohol Content (BAC) is the percentage of alcohol in a person's bloodstream. First use in any context (onboarding, first session, first notification) must include this explanation. Never assume the user knows what BAC means. The phrase "standard drink" (which varies by country and is statistically misunderstood) should be avoided or always immediately defined. Use "drink you logged" instead.

### 2.2 — Voice Spectrum

```
CASUAL ◄────────────────────────────────────► CLINICAL
        ▲                                    ▲
        │  Social Media                      │  Medical Export
        │  Onboarding Welcome                │  EHR Summaries
        │  Milestone Celebrations            │  Lab-context Screens
        │                                    │
        │           WARM ◄──► DIRECT         │
        │                                    │
        │  Recovery Support                  │  BAC Status Messages
        │  Morning-After Summary             │  Medication Warnings
        │  Sobriety Streaks                  │  Legal Disclaimers
        │                                    │
```

**The Default Zone:** SipTrack lives in the center — calm, informative, factual. We move toward warm for recovery contexts, toward clinical for medical contexts, but we never reach either extreme.

### 2.3 — Copywriting Guide by Context

#### Context 1: Active Session
**Tone:** Calm, factual, nonjudgmental
**Core principle:** Give data. Don't interpret it.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "You've had 3 drinks. Estimated BAC: 0.06%"
- ✅ "Session: 2h 14m | Drinks: 4 | Est. BAC: 0.08%"
- ❌ "Whoa, you've been at it a while!" (editorializing)
- ❌ "Warning: You've consumed a lot tonight." (shaming)

**Formula:** [Metric] + [Number] + [Optional: next threshold if relevant]
**Max copy length:** 1 headline + 1 supporting line. No paragraphs.

---

#### Context 2: Morning-After Summary
**Tone:** Supportive, informative, matter-of-fact
**Core principle:** Report what happened. Don't editorialize.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "Last night: 4 drinks over 3 hours. Peak BAC: 0.09%."
- ✅ "Session ended at 11:42 PM. You were back to 0.00% by 4:15 AM."
- ❌ "Looks like you had a rough night!" (false cheer, editorializing)
- ❌ "You may want to take it easy today." (prescriptive)

**Formula:** [What happened] + [When] + [Key metric]. Passive reporting, not active advice.
**Max copy length:** 2–3 short sentences. Bullet-friendly.

---

#### Context 3: Sobriety Streak
**Tone:** Celebratory, warm, personal
**Core principle:** Celebrate the milestone. Never mention what they're abstaining from.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "Day 42. You're doing something meaningful."
- ✅ "One year. 365 days of data, logged by you."
- ❌ "42 days without alcohol!" (names what they're avoiding — can feel shame-inducing)
- ❌ "You're crushing your sobriety goals!" (assumes framing; "sobriety" may not be their word)

**Formula:** [Number] + [Time unit] + [Warm, brief affirmation — not congratulations, not cheerleading]
**Max copy length:** 2 lines. Quiet, warm, not loud.

---

#### Context 4: Medication Warning
**Tone:** Direct, urgent, clear
**Core principle:** Life-safety context. No ambiguity allowed.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "Alcohol may interact with [Medication Name]. Wait until BAC: 0.00%"
- ✅ "Your BAC is 0.04%. [Medication] is not recommended above 0.00%."
- ❌ "Be careful mixing alcohol with your meds!" (too casual for life-safety)
- ❌ "You really shouldn't drink with this medication." (prescriptive, shaming)

**Formula:** [Interaction fact] + [Current BAC] + [Action: wait for BAC 0.00%]
**Max copy length:** 2 short sentences. Bold or highlighted. No softening language.

---

#### Context 5: Marketing
**Tone:** Confident, inclusive, benefit-driven
**Core principle:** Speak to awareness, not drinking or not-drinking.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "Your relationship with alcohol is yours. We just give you the data."
- ✅ "SipTrack tracks what matters to you. Nothing more."
- ❌ "Track your drinks and drink smarter!" (implies a drinking lifestyle)
- ❌ "The sober app for people who care about their health." (alienates active drinkers)

**Formula:** [Empowerment statement] + [What we offer] + [CTA]

---

#### Context 6: App Store
**Tone:** Benefit-driven, concise, search-optimized
**Core principle:** Lead with safety and tracking; avoid niche signals that repel breadth.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "Know your BAC (Blood Alcohol Content) in real time. Make informed decisions."
- ✅ "BAC tracker • Safe-to-drive timer • Sobriety streaks • Apple Watch support"
- ❌ "The ultimate party companion!" (glamorizes; repels recovery users)
- ❌ "For people struggling with alcohol." (repels active drinkers; inaccurate framing)

**Formula:** Lead benefit + Feature bullets + Privacy/data statement + CTA

---

#### Context 7: Medical / Professional
**Tone:** Professional, precise, standards-compliant
**Core principle:** Speak to professionals in professional language. No hedging.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "SipTrack provides patient-reported alcohol intake data compatible with EHR export."
- ✅ "Widmark formula-based BAC estimation. Patient weight, sex, and hydration inputs factored."
- ❌ "Doctors love SipTrack!" (vague, unverifiable)
- ❌ "Great for patients who drink too much." (stigmatizing, imprecise)

**Formula:** [Capability] + [Standard or protocol referenced] + [Clinical use case]

---

#### Context 8: Recovery Support
**Tone:** Warm, human, completely non-preachy
**Core principle:** The user's relationship with alcohol is private. We reference streaks and milestones, never the word "sober" unless the user has identified that mode.
**Examples:**
- ✅ "7 days. Every single one counted."
- ✅ "Your streak is yours. We're just keeping track."
- ❌ "Stay strong! You're beating addiction!" (dramatic, presumptuous)
- ❌ "Keep going — sobriety is worth it!" (prescriptive; assumes value system)

**Formula:** [Metric] + [Warm, one-sentence affirmation — first or second person, understated]

---

### 2.4 — Say This / Not That

| ✅ Say This | ❌ Not That | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Estimated BAC: 0.06%" | "You're a little drunk." | Numbers over interpretation |
| "4 drinks logged." | "You've been drinking heavily." | Neutral metric, not judgment |
| "Day 30." | "30 days sober!" | Doesn't name what they're abstaining from |
| "Know your number." | "Drink smarter." | "Drink smarter" implies drinking; excludes recovery users |
| "Back to 0.00% by 3:15 AM." | "You'll be sober by 3:15 AM." | "Sober" is identity-loaded; "0.00%" is a fact |
| "BAC (Blood Alcohol Content)" | "BAC" (first use) | Never assume the user knows the acronym |
| "You've had 2 drinks in the last hour." | "Slow down." | Information, not instruction |
| "Track your personal limit." | "Set your drinking limit." | "Personal limit" works for all users including non-drinkers |
| "Session complete." | "Drinking session over." | "Drinking session" excludes non-drinking users |
| "Safe-to-drive estimate: 11:30 PM" | "You can drive after 11:30." | "Estimate" reflects scientific humility; "can" implies certainty |

---

### 2.5 — Legal Disclaimer Template

The following disclaimer must appear adjacent to every BAC reading displayed in the app:

> **BAC Estimate Disclaimer:**
> This BAC estimate is calculated using the Widmark formula and is based on your body weight, biological sex, drinks logged, and session duration. It is an estimate only and may not reflect your actual blood alcohol level. Individual factors including metabolism, food consumption, medications, health conditions, and other variables can significantly affect actual BAC. Do not rely on this estimate to determine your fitness to drive or operate machinery. Always err on the side of caution. If in doubt, do not drive. SipTrack is not a medical device and this information is not medical advice.

**Short-form version (for small screen contexts, e.g., Apple Watch):**
> *Estimated BAC. Not medical advice. Do not rely on this to decide to drive.*

## 3. Color System

### 3.1 — Design Principles

- Every color pair used in UI must achieve WCAG AAA contrast (7:1 for normal text, 4.5:1 for large text ≥18pt or ≥14pt bold)
- Every color has a verified dark mode equivalent
- No color shall carry cultural alcohol associations (no beer amber primary, no wine red primary)
- The palette works in both bright sunlight and dim bar lighting (Jorge Test)
- Colors are gender-neutral — no skew toward traditionally masculine or feminine associations

## 4. Typography System

### 4.1 — Font Selection

**Primary Font: SF Pro (iOS) / Roboto (Android)**

**Rationale:**
System fonts are the correct choice for SipTrack for four reasons:
1. **Performance:** Zero loading time. Renders at every supported device size without a font request.
2. **Localization:** System fonts support all required languages and scripts automatically.
3. **Accessibility:** SF Pro integrates with iOS Dynamic Type and VoiceOver without custom configuration.
4. **Familiarity:** Users trust their device's native type. It signals "this belongs on my phone."

**Font Weights Used:**
- SF Pro / Roboto **Ultralight (100):** Display numbers only (BAC Number style)
- SF Pro / Roboto **Light (300):** Display Large style
- SF Pro / Roboto **Regular (400):** Body text, captions, labels
- SF Pro / Roboto **Medium (500):** Secondary headings, navigation labels, button labels
- SF Pro / Roboto **Semibold (600):** Heading 1, Heading 2, key data points
- SF Pro / Roboto **Bold (700):** Heading 3, critical warnings, notification bold text

**Fallback Stack (web/cross-platform):**
`"SF Pro Display", "SF Pro Text", "Roboto", -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif`

## 5. Iconography System

### 5.1 — Icon Style Guide

**Style:** Rounded-line (outlined, with filled variants for selected/active states)
**Line Weight:** 1.5pt stroke at 24pt canvas (scales proportionally)
**Corner Radius:** 2pt on square caps; 100% on terminals (rounded ends)
**Grid Size:** 24pt × 24pt base canvas; 28pt × 28pt for tab bar; 20pt × 20pt for list items
**Padding within grid:** 2pt padding from grid edge on all sides (usable area: 20pt × 20pt on 24pt grid)

**Filled vs. Outlined:**
- **Outlined:** Default / unselected state
- **Filled:** Selected / active state (tab bar active, toggle on)
- **Filled with color:** Status icons (BAC Safe = filled green, BAC Danger = filled red)

**Optical alignment:** Not mathematical — optically centered. Circles appear smaller than squares at same bounding box; compensate by sizing circles 2pt larger than squares.

## 6. Spacing & Layout System

### 6.1 — Base Unit

**4pt grid system.** All spacing values are multiples of 4pt. This grid is universal across iOS (pt) and Android (dp — effectively equivalent for layout purposes at 1× density).

**Why 4pt:** SF Pro's metrics align to 4pt. Apple HIG implicitly uses 4pt. Android Material Design uses 4dp. 8pt is common but 4pt gives finer control for the density of BAC data displays.

### 6.5 — Card Elevation / Shadow Specifications

## 7. Logo Specifications

### 7.1 — Logo Concept

**Concept: The Clarity Arc**

The SipTrack logo is a precise arc — a partial circle spanning approximately 270 degrees, open at the bottom-right. Within the arc sits a single downward-pointing chevron or tick mark at the center-bottom of the arc, suggesting a gauge needle at rest or at zero. The wordmark "SipTrack" sits in a horizontal lockup to the right of (or below, in stacked variant) this symbol.

The arc is drawn with the brand Primary Indigo color. It is not a speedometer needle — the arc itself IS the gauge, which is also the hero UI element of the app. The logo and the app's central metaphor are the same visual language.

The single tick (chevron) at the arc's nadir is the "needle at rest" — a suggestion of measurement, precision, readiness.

There are no beverage containers, no bubbles, no liquid, no grain, no hops, no grapes. The shape is entirely abstract and technological.

**Wordmark:** "SipTrack" in SF Pro Display Semibold, tracked slightly (letter-spacing: +0.02em). The capital S and T are the brand's monogram anchor. Not styled or modified beyond standard type setting.

### 7.5 — Clear Space Rule

Minimum clear space on all sides = **height of the uppercase "S" in "SipTrack" in the wordmark.**

For icon-only mark: minimum clear space on all sides = **1× the width of the arc stroke.**

This clear space must be maintained against all adjacent content, photography, background color changes, and screen edges.

---

### 7.6 — Logo Misuse Guide

The following are NEVER permitted:

1. **Do not add color gradients to the arc.** The arc is a flat, single-color stroke. No rainbow, no metallic, no shine.
2. **Do not add drop shadows to the logo.** The logo is not an element to be visually lifted — it is a mark.
3. **Do not place the logo on a mid-tone background.** Light logo on dark. Dark logo on light. Never on grey, never on color without contrast verification.
4. **Do not stretch or distort the logo.** Lock the aspect ratio at all times.
5. **Do not modify the arc angle.** The 270° arc with nadir tick is the logo. Adjusting it changes the meaning.
6. **Do not use the wordmark without the icon mark in app contexts.** The mark and wordmark are a system.
7. **Do not add taglines, straplines, or other text inside the clear space.** Taglines live outside the safe zone.
8. **Do not apply photographic or patterned backgrounds behind the logo without contrast verification.** The logo must pass 7:1 contrast against its immediate background.

## 8. Photography & Imagery Style

### 8.1 — Photography Subjects and Compositions

SipTrack imagery shows people in moments of quiet self-awareness. Not parties. Not bars. Not restraint.

**Approved scenes:**
- A person checking their phone quietly at a restaurant table (no drinks visible)
- Hands holding a phone with a visible gauge or number (not showing what they drank)
- A person in athletic wear reviewing data on their watch
- Someone at home, morning light, reviewing a session summary on their phone — coffee or tea visible
- A healthcare professional (nurse, doctor) reviewing data on a tablet in a clinical setting
- A family at a dinner table — no emphasis on alcohol, just togetherness
- A person alone, comfortable, at peace — reading their phone in a quiet setting
- An older adult comfortably using a phone — large type visible, comfortable grip

**Composition principles:**
- Natural, candid framing — not posed, not stock-photo stiff
- Rule of thirds — person slightly off-center; phone or watch visible but not filling the frame
- Phone/watch screen: shows simplified UI mockup (gauge, a number) — never shows drink icons prominently
- Negative space: images should feel airy, not cluttered

---

### 8.2 — Diversity Requirements

Every image asset set used in marketing must collectively represent:

- **Age range:** At least one image per major age band: 19–30, 31–45, 46–60, 60+
- **Ethnicity:** Minimum representation required: Black/African American, East Asian, South Asian, Hispanic/Latino, White/European, at least one Middle Eastern or Indigenous representation across the full set
- **Body types:** Diverse body sizes represented — no model-only aesthetic
- **Abilities:** At minimum one image featuring visible adaptive technology or disability (wheelchair, hearing aid, etc.) across the marketing asset set
- **Gender:** Images must be distributed across all gender presentations — no male-dominated or female-dominated set
- **Settings:** Urban, suburban, and rural settings represented across the full set

---

### 8.3 — Mood and Lighting Guidelines

**Mood:** Composed, calm, private, self-assured. Not celebratory, not worried.

**Lighting:**
- Natural light preferred: golden hour morning (hopeful), soft overcast (balanced), office daylight
- Avoid: flash photography, neon bar lighting, dark dramatic lighting
- Color temperature: 5000–6000K (daylight neutral). Not warm-filter ("Instagram"), not cool-filter (clinical/cold)
- Shadows: Soft and directional — no hard shadows (creates tension; fails the calm brand principle)

**Post-processing:**
- Minimal, true-to-life
- Slight lift to shadows (ensures legibility on dark screens)
- No vignettes, no tinted filters, no skin smoothing
- Brand colors should not bleed into photography — photos are neutral; UI elements carry the brand color

---

### 8.4 — What to NEVER Show in Imagery

- **Alcoholic beverages** — no beer bottles, wine glasses, spirits, cocktails. Not "in the background."
- **Consumption acts** — no one drinking, no one pouring
- **Wasted/impaired states** — no disheveled appearances, no drooping eyes, no "drunk" body language
- **Shame cues** — no hiding, no furtive glances, no looking guilty about phone use
- **Bars or liquor stores as primary settings** — these may appear in the very far background of a candid but should not be the scene
- **Pills or syringes** — the medication feature is not represented photographically with drug paraphernalia
- **Any cultural stereotype** — no predictable alcohol-culture stereotyping (e.g., frat culture, "wine mom," etc.)

---

### 8.5 — Illustration Style

When photography is not available or appropriate, SipTrack uses a minimal line-illustration style:

- **Style:** Clean vector, flat (no shadows or 3D), 2pt line weight
- **Color:** Illustrations use 2–3 colors maximum, always from the brand palette
- **Characters:** Abstract body representations — oval heads, no facial details — to maximize inclusivity (no ethnicity, no gender assumed)
- **Subject matter:** UI metaphors (data flowing, a shield for privacy, a chart growing) rather than alcohol/drinking scenarios
- **Usage:** Onboarding screens, empty states, marketing explainer sections only — not in active data screens

## 9. Motion & Animation Principles

### 9.1 — Core Motion Principles

**Principle 1: Purposeful, Not Decorative**
Every animation in SipTrack carries information. The BAC gauge sweeping to a new value shows change over time. A modal sliding up shows depth/hierarchy. Nothing moves for the sake of moving. If removing an animation would not affect user comprehension, the animation should be removed.

**Principle 2: Calm Velocity**
Animation timing defaults to 300–400ms with ease-in-out curves. Nothing snaps. Nothing jolts. The app's animated body language mirrors its verbal language — unhurried, confident, settled. Avoid spring physics that overshoot dramatically.

**Principle 3: Honesty About Change**
When a value changes (BAC number updates, streak day increments), the transition communicates what changed — not just that something changed. BAC going up animates differently than BAC going down. The direction of the arc sweep communicates meaning.

**Principle 4: Accessibility First, Then Delight**
Every animation has a functional equivalent for users with Reduced Motion enabled (iOS: Settings → Accessibility → Reduce Motion). Delight layers can be removed; the information layer must remain. The app is fully functional and comprehensible with all animation disabled.

**Principle 5: Conservative, Not Playful**
SipTrack is not a game. The animation vocabulary is professional — closer to a Bloomberg terminal than to a fitness app. No confetti. No bouncing. No rubber-band effects on primary elements. Reserved celebrations (a gentle shimmer, a single pulse) are acceptable for milestone moments only.

---

### 9.2 — BAC Gauge Animation Behavior

The circular gauge arc is the hero animation in the app.

**On session start:**
- Arc starts at 0° (nadir)
- Duration: 600ms ease-out to starting BAC estimate
- Color: begins as BAC Safe Green, transitions to Caution or Danger based on value
- Number: count-up from 0.00 to current value, matching arc timing

**On BAC update (drink logged):**
- Arc sweeps clockwise to new position
- Duration: 800ms ease-in-out
- If crossing a threshold (safe→caution, caution→danger): color transition begins at midpoint of arc sweep
- Number: crossfade (fade out old, fade in new) at 400ms midpoint
- Haptic: medium impact at arc arrival

**On BAC decrease (metabolism, time passage):**
- Arc sweeps counter-clockwise
- Duration: 600ms ease-in-out
- Color reversal follows the same threshold rules
- Haptic: light impact when crossing from danger→caution; soft notification when reaching 0.00%

**Reduced Motion fallback:**
- Arc: instantaneous jump to new value (no animation)
- Number: crossfade remains (reduced from 400ms to 150ms)
- Color: instantaneous change
- Haptic: unchanged (haptics are not "motion" and should remain)

---

### 9.3 — Transition Types

**Page Transitions:**
- Standard push (deeper in hierarchy): slide left — 300ms, ease-in-out
- Back (returning): slide right — 250ms, ease-out
- Modal presentation: slide up from bottom — 350ms, ease-out; background dims to 40% opacity overlay
- Modal dismiss: slide down — 280ms, ease-in

**Reduced Motion fallback for page transitions:**
- Crossfade only — 200ms opacity fade. No position changes.

**Haptic Feedback Pairings:**

| BAC reaches threshold | UINotificationFeedbackGenerator | Warning |

| Safe to drive | UINotificationFeedbackGenerator | Success |
| Medication warning activated | UINotificationFeedbackGenerator | Error |

| Streak milestone | UINotificationFeedbackGenerator | Success |
